## CAD Creative Project Overview

For MAE 2250, I modeled an Apple Watch and sports band assembly as an individual CAD design project. The goal was to recreate the product using separate components that could be assembled together while demonstrating careful control over geometry, features, and part relationships.

## Modeling Approach

I modeled the Apple Watch as a set of separate parts rather than using a top-down model. The main body was built first using sketching and extrusions, then I added detailed features such as the digital crown, side button, band slots, and back sensors using split bodies, extrude and pull operations, and lofting for the curved sensor geometry. Fillets were used to round the edges and improve the overall appearance of the watch body.

## CAD Learning and Strategy

The modeling strategy focused on building a stable base geometry first and then layering more detailed features onto it. Creating the main body before the bands and smaller components made the assembly process cleaner and more accurate, especially since the design was meant to represent a product with individually removable parts. The project also strengthened my understanding of modular design and introduced more advanced CAD tools such as loft, patch, and stitch for smoother transitions between surfaces.

## Personal Contributions

Because this was an individual project, all of the CAD modeling, feature creation, and assembly work was my own. I designed the main body, band components, crown, buttons, and sensor geometry, and I also prepared the sketch and rendering views used to present the final model.
