## Sustainable Mobility Project Overview


For the Cornell University Sustainable Design program, my team worked to rethink the transit network of Tompkins County through a systems design approach. The project combined design thinking, systems engineering, and empathy-driven fieldwork to develop practical, user-centered solutions for local transportation challenges. By integrating perspectives from architecture, engineering, and planning, the team aimed to create a sustainable mobility concept that was both technically feasible and responsive to community needs.


## System Approach


The project was organized around a cross-disciplinary workflow that blended user research with structured systems methods. The team used fieldwork, stakeholder feedback, and systems engineering tools to define the transit problem, evaluate design tradeoffs, and guide concept development. This approach allowed the team to move from broad mobility goals toward more detailed design and implementation decisions while keeping the needs of Tompkins County residents at the center of the process.


## Hardware Design


A major focus of the project was the development of a modular bus shelter system. The design evolved from architectural concept models into high-fidelity engineering validation, including rebuilt CAD geometry, simplified module forms for analysis, and refined dovetail and finger-joint connections. The shelter structure used a catenary-inspired form, Black Locust wood components, polycarbonate window panels, and a steel base assembly designed for durability, manufacturability, and long-term outdoor performance.


## Software and Logic


The project used engineering analysis and systems planning to support design decisions. CAD models were transferred into simulation-ready formats for Ansys, while simplified geometry and standardized dimensions were used to improve meshing and structural evaluation. The team also developed base, electrical, and modular connection logic to support LED integration, weather protection, and future smart transit features, including considerations for routing, waterproofing, and modular assembly [file:1].


## Performance and Results


Finite element analysis and hand calculations were used to validate the shelter under dead load and snow load conditions. The team reported manageable deformation under dead loading and stronger stress responses under snow loading, while identifying key reinforcement areas such as base support placement and joint interfaces. These results helped confirm the structural direction of the shelter while also highlighting future improvements for load transfer, waterproofing, and modular scalability [file:1].


## Personal Contributions


My contributions focused on mechanical design, CAD development, and structural analysis support. I helped refine the modular geometry for simulation, supported joint and base redesigns, and contributed to the integration of electrical cutouts and polycarbonate window features. I also assisted with FEA interpretation, documentation, and the translation of architectural concepts into a mechanically viable shelter system [file:1].
