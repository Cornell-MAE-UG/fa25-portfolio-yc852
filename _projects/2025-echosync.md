## EchoSync Project Overview


For SYSEN 5900, my team designed EchoSync, a wearable safety system intended to support student well-being through discreet peer-based alerting. The project focused on moments when students feel discomfort, uncertainty, or vulnerability in everyday campus settings, especially when phone-based safety tools are difficult to access or socially conspicuous. EchoSync was developed as a bracelet-based system that combines human-centered design, systems engineering, and iterative prototyping to create a more intuitive and emotionally supportive safety solution.


## System Approach


EchoSync was designed as a peer-connected safety ecosystem rather than a standalone emergency device. When the user intentionally activates the bracelet, the system sends a silent alert to trusted friends through both short-range bracelet-to-bracelet communication and longer-range phone-connected notification pathways. This hybrid architecture was intended to preserve discretion while still allowing location sharing and coordinated support when needed.


## Hardware Design


The physical design centered on a wearable bracelet form factor that balanced comfort, discretion, and reliability. Early prototypes used thrifted bracelet mockups to test wearability and user interaction, while later versions evolved into a beaded bracelet aesthetic with a deliberate slide-based activation mechanism. The final design also included interchangeable bands and a refined housing layout, with finite element analysis used to assess structural integrity under normal and worst-case loading conditions.


## Software and Logic


The system logic was built around intentional activation, haptic confirmation, and coordinated notification handling. User research showed that the bracelet needed to be easy to use under stress but resistant to accidental triggering, so the activation mechanism was designed to require a deliberate sliding motion. The software and interaction flow were supported by use case diagrams, activity diagrams, and systems modeling tools that defined how the user, the bracelet, and trusted contacts interact during a safety event.


## Performance and Results


Testing and prototyping helped validate the core design direction. Users consistently preferred discreet haptic feedback over visible lights, and they valued a form factor that looked more like everyday jewelry than a safety device. Structural analysis showed that the housing remained robust during normal activation loading, while worst-case tensile loading identified the strap attachment as a future reinforcement area. Overall, the project demonstrated that EchoSync could support a feasible and user-centered approach to campus safety.
