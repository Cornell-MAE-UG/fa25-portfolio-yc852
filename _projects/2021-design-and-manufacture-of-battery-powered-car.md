## Battery Powered Car Project Overview

For ENGRI 1170, my team designed, manufactured, and tested a battery-powered car that balanced speed, pulling force, manufacturability, and competition constraints. The car was built from laser-cut basswood and acrylic, powered by a motor and gearbox, and tuned to perform well in both the speed and torque challenges while staying within the project’s weight and geometry limits.

## Design Approach

Our design evolved through multiple iterations before we settled on the final version. We started with a flip-able two-wheel concept that could switch between speed and torque configurations, then moved to a single-gearbox, single-wheel design with a fixed center of mass and a more streamlined body. The final design improved on that second concept by adding a roof, inserting higher-density acrylic in cutouts to increase weight and traction, and using acrylic bushings to reduce axle friction.

## Manufacturing and Build

Manufacturing was centered on laser cutting, assembly, soldering, and iterative adjustments during fabrication. The team reported that the laser-cut parts fit well, the gearbox and motor assembly went smoothly, and the acrylic bushings were produced successfully, though one had to be remade after it was lost. Small build changes were made to improve performance, including adding tabs for stronger glue joints, dropping the front axle for ground clearance, increasing ballast near the rear axle, and reversing the motor and gearbox wiring after the drivetrain was installed upside down.

## Analysis and Results

The design calculations estimated an optimized gear ratio of 13.14, a top speed of 4.25 m/s, and a maximum pulling force of 4.7 N. In competition, the car achieved an average speed of 2.94 m/s, a top speed of 5.89 m/s, and pulling forces of 2.49 N and 2.09 N across the two trials, placing second in the speed event but underperforming in pulling force relative to the prediction. The report attributes the gap mainly to a lower-than-expected coefficient of friction, late design changes that shifted the center of mass, and general drivetrain losses.

## Personal Contributions

My main contributions were in design development, calculations, and report preparation. I helped shape the evolution from the early flip-able layout to the final single-orientation car, supported the gear ratio and performance calculations, and contributed to the manufacturing decisions that improved traction, clearance, and axle performance. I also helped document the process, compare predicted versus measured performance, and identify the main error sources that affected the pulling-force results.
